### 1. WHAT IS IT?

The safety-versus-capability trade-off is the tension that making a model refuse or constrain more harmful requests tends to also reduce its helpfulness on legitimate ones (over-refusal), and that safer is not automatically less capable nor more capable automatically riskier.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

Evaluating only harm or only helpfulness hides the cost of the other. Decisions need the frontier between them.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

- Any classifier-style safety mechanism has a threshold: stricter means more harmful requests refused (higher safety recall) and more benign ones refused (lower helpfulness). Figure 10 sweeps it on toy risk scores.
- Plot the **frontier**: helpfulness (benign requests answered) vs safety (harmful refused) for each model or setting; better systems move the whole curve up and right, not just along it.
- Evaluate **borderline** and **dual-use** prompts separately: they carry most of the trade-off.
- Capability itself can raise risk (more able to help a bad actor) and enable safer behaviour (better at recognising harm); both effects are empirical and model-specific (REVIEW_REQUIRED).
- The chosen operating point is a policy decision depending on severity of harm, user population and mitigations around the model (Tier 32).
- Compare models at equal safety or equal helpfulness; otherwise differences are only threshold shifts.

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

Compute helpfulness and safety at several thresholds or system prompts -> plot frontier -> choose point with stakeholders -> encode as gates on both axes ([15.32 Evaluation gates](15.32-evaluation-gates.md)).

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Airport security: stricter screening catches more threats but delays and inconveniences more innocent travellers.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

Toy risk scores: threshold 0.5 refuses about 87% of harmful and 22% of benign requests; threshold 0.7 refuses about 48% / 3% (run the snippet; the data are synthetic and illustrative, see Figure 10).

### 7. CODE

```python
# run inside lab/ -- trace a toy safety/helpfulness frontier
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
harmful = rng.random(4000) < 0.3
risk = np.clip(rng.normal(np.where(harmful, 0.7, 0.35), 0.18), 0, 1)
for t in (0.3, 0.5, 0.7):
    print(f"refuse if risk >= {t}: harmful refused {(risk[harmful] >= t).mean():.2f}, benign answered {(risk[~harmful] < t).mean():.2f}")
```

### 8. INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT

```
threshold / policy setting -> refusal decisions on benign + harmful sets -> (helpfulness, safety) point -> frontier -> chosen operating point
```

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

Policy teams choose operating points per product and audience (for example stricter for a children's app); gates encode both safety and over-refusal bounds.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Frames alignment (Tier 10) and guardrail (Tier 32) design as movements along or of the frontier.

### 11. ADVANTAGES

- Exposes costs on both sides; supports explicit choice.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

- Toy frontiers differ from real ones; labels for borderline cases are contested.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

- The trade-off itself: harm avoidance vs helpfulness; automation vs human review of borderline cases.

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

- Reporting refusal rate as pure safety.
- Comparing models at different operating points.
- Ignoring borderline prompts.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Needs both benign and harmful evaluation sets; borderline sets need expert labels.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Over-restrictive systems push users to less safe alternatives; under-restrictive ones enable misuse; both are risks.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

Safer settings usually refuse more benign requests too, so I evaluate harm avoidance and over-refusal together as a frontier, compare systems at equal operating points or by curve dominance, treat borderline prompts separately, and encode both axes as release gates.

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

Plot the frontier; compare at equal points; gate both axes.

### 19. INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

- **Basic:** What is over-refusal?
- **Intermediate:** How do you compare two models fairly on this trade-off?
- **Advanced:** Does more capability mean less safety?
- **Scenario:** A stricter filter cut harmful outputs 40% but support tickets about refusals rose 3x; decision?
