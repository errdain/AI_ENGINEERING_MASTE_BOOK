### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Whisper (OpenAI, released 2022) is a family of open-weight encoder-decoder Transformer speech models trained with large-scale weak supervision on multilingual, multitask data. It does transcription, translation to English, language identification and timestamping from one model. This file is a case study: **all specifics below are `REVIEW_REQUIRED`**.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

Before Whisper, strong ASR needed carefully curated, domain-specific data. The Whisper work argued that scaling weakly labelled, diverse web audio yields a robust general model without per-dataset fine-tuning.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

**Reported design (`REVIEW_REQUIRED`: verify against the paper and model cards).**
- *Data:* about 680 000 hours of audio with transcripts collected from the internet, multilingual (roughly a third non-English audio) and multitask.
- *Input:* audio resampled to 16 kHz, split into 30 s windows, converted to log-mel (80 channels; some later versions 128).
- *Architecture:* standard encoder-decoder Transformer; convolutional stem then Transformer encoder; decoder with cross-attention ([14.11](14.11-encoder-decoder-asr.md)).
- *Multitask format:* special tokens tell the decoder what to do: start-of-transcript, language tag, task (transcribe or translate), timestamps on/off, then text tokens, then end-of-text.
- *Sizes:* from tiny (tens of millions of parameters) to large (over a billion), plus 'turbo'/distilled variants; check current model cards.

**Engineering consequences.** (1) Fixed 30 s windows: pad short audio, chunk long audio with sliding windows and context from the previous chunk; (2) timestamps are predicted as special tokens in 20 ms steps and are approximate, so word-level timing is often obtained with a separate alignment step ([14.15](14.15-timestamps-alignment.md)); (3) known failure modes: hallucinated or repeated text on silence/music, language-ID mistakes, drift on long audio; mitigate with VAD ([14.13](14.13-speech-segmentation.md)), temperature fallback and compression-ratio checks.

**Where it fits in this tier.** It is the concrete instance of [14.5](14.05-mel-spectrograms.md) (log-mel front-end) plus [14.11](14.11-encoder-decoder-asr.md) (architecture) plus [14.13](14.13-speech-segmentation.md) (VAD for long audio). The lab does not reimplement Whisper; it uses a toy CTC model to teach the pieces.

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

```
audio ─► 16 kHz ─► 30 s window ─► log-mel [80,3000] ─► encoder ─► E
 decoder prompt: <|startoftranscript|><|en|><|transcribe|><|notimestamps|> ─► text tokens ─► <|endoftext|>
```
(Token names above follow the published format; **REVIEW_REQUIRED**.)

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

One interpreter who has listened to a huge amount of everyday speech in many languages: not perfect in any technical jargon, but surprisingly capable everywhere without special training.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

A transcription service wraps Whisper: VAD splits audio, each speech segment is padded to 30 s, decoding uses beam search with temperature fallback, word timestamps come from cross-attention/DTW alignment or a separate aligner, and WER is measured on the customer's own audio before launch.

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

Used (via open weights or hosted APIs) for captioning, call analytics and voice notes; enterprises add domain vocabulary prompts, PII redaction, human review and monitoring of hallucinations on silence.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Often the ASR stage of cascaded voice agents ([14.19](14.19-realtime-speech-systems.md)) and of audio-RAG pipelines ([14.29](14.29-audio-video-text-alignment.md)).

### 11. ADVANTAGES

Robust to accents/noise relative to narrow-domain systems; multilingual; open weights; multi-task.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

Not streaming by design; fixed windows; hallucination on non-speech; timestamps imprecise; weaker on low-resource languages and heavy jargon; model details change across versions.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

Model size vs speed vs accuracy; hosted API convenience vs data privacy and cost; fine-tuning for domain vs robustness.

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

- Feeding silence/music without VAD.
- Assuming word timestamps are exact.
- Quoting benchmark WERs without the normaliser used.
- Using the wrong language setting.
- Treating one model version's behaviour as permanent.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Cost grows with audio duration and model size; large models need a GPU for good throughput. Batch offline jobs; quantised/distilled variants trade a little accuracy for speed (`REVIEW_REQUIRED` for numbers).

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Transcribing recordings may breach consent/retention rules; hosted APIs send audio off-premises. Hallucinated transcripts can fabricate statements attributed to a speaker: keep audio as source of truth and log model versions.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

*"Whisper is a Transformer encoder-decoder trained on a very large weakly supervised multilingual dataset, taking 30-second log-mel windows and using special tokens to select language, task and timestamps. It is robust and multi-task, but not streaming, can hallucinate on silence, and its timestamps are coarse. In production I add VAD, chunking, an alignment step and evaluation on the customer's own audio."*
