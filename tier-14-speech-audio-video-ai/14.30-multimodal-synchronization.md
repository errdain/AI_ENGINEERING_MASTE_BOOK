### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Multimodal synchronization keeps audio, video and text on the same time axis: correct offsets between tracks, consistent clocks across devices, and lip-sync tolerances at playback. Drift or constant offset makes lips and voice disagree and breaks alignment-based features.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

Humans notice audio-visual offsets of a few tens of milliseconds; models trained on synchronized data degrade when inputs are misaligned; multi-device recordings drift because clocks differ.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

**Sources of offset.** Different capture latencies (camera vs microphone), encoder/muxer delays, network jitter and buffering, variable frame rate, clock drift between devices (e.g. 50 ppm drift is 180 ms per hour; arithmetic, drift specs vary), re-encoding with trimmed audio priming samples.

**Human tolerance.** Detectability thresholds are on the order of tens of milliseconds, asymmetric: audio-ahead is noticed at smaller offsets than audio-behind. Broadcast recommendations quote about +45 ms (audio early) and -125 ms (audio late) (`REVIEW_REQUIRED`: verify against the ITU document before citing).

**Estimating offset by cross-correlation (implemented).** Build a per-frame audio loudness envelope and a visual activity signal (e.g. mouth-region motion, or frame difference), resample both to a common rate, and find the lag that maximises their normalised cross-correlation (`av_offset_estimate`). Learned approaches such as SyncNet-style audio-visual models score sync from lips and sound (`REVIEW_REQUIRED`).

**Fixes.** Apply a constant offset (trim/pad audio), correct drift with resampling at a slightly different rate, use shared timestamps (PTS/NTP/PTP) rather than file order, and test with a clap/flash sync signal.

**For model pipelines.** Always convert everything to seconds on a shared clock before alignment ([14.29](14.29-audio-video-text-alignment.md)) and store the offsets used.

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

Explore: [explore: `latency_sync_cost.html`](interactive/latency_sync_cost.html) (section 3: slide the A/V offset and see the ms, frames and whether it is likely noticeable).

```
audio envelope a[t] ─┐
                     ├─► cross-correlate over lags ─► argmax lag = offset (in frames/ms) ─► shift track
video activity v[t] ─┘
```

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

A badly dubbed film: the voice comes a beat after the lips move. Synchronisation is nudging the sound track earlier or later until they line up.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

The lab test delays a random envelope by 7 samples and recovers lag = -7 (convention in the docstring: negative means the video track is delayed relative to the audio).

### 7. CODE

```python
# run inside lab/
import numpy as np
from video_core import av_offset_estimate

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
audio_env = rng.random(200)                       # e.g. loudness per video frame
video_env = np.roll(audio_env, 7)                 # video activity delayed by 7 frames
lag = av_offset_estimate(audio_env, video_env, max_lag=20)
print(lag, "frames =", round(abs(lag) / 30 * 1000), "ms at 30 fps")   # -7: negative lag = video delayed relative to audio
```

```
tracks (a, v, text) + clocks ─► offset/drift estimation ─► correction ─► synchronised timeline ─► playback / models
```

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

Broadcasters and conferencing vendors run lip-sync monitoring; multi-camera productions use timecode or clap markers; ML teams verify sync of training data.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Misaligned audio-video training pairs teach models wrong correlations; sync verification is part of dataset QA for audio-visual models and for avatar/lip-sync generation.

### 11. ADVANTAGES

Cheap to estimate; improves perceived quality and model accuracy.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

Correlation fails with little visual-audio coupling (no faces, silence); variable offsets need windowed estimates.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

Windowed (adaptive) estimation tracks drift but is noisier than a global offset.

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

- Assuming container timestamps are correct.
- Fixing offset in the wrong direction (check sign).
- Ignoring drift on long recordings.
- Syncing at the wrong sample/frame rate.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Cross-correlation is O(N x lags); use FFT-based correlation for long signals.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Desynchronisation can be a deepfake tell (and deepfake generators now fix it): do not use sync alone as authenticity proof (`REVIEW_REQUIRED`).
