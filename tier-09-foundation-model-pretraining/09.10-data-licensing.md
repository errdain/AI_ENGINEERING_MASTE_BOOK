### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Managing the legal terms attached to training text: permissive, share-alike, non-commercial, "all rights reserved" or none stated, plus opt-outs and takedowns.

> This is engineering guidance, **not legal advice**. Whether training on a given text is permitted depends on jurisdiction, license terms and ongoing legal developments. Every policy statement here is `REVIEW_REQUIRED` by counsel.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

Text is usually copyrighted. Training on it without a defensible policy exposes the organisation to legal, reputational and contractual risk, and to takedown demands you cannot honour without metadata.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

Engineering consequences: keep license metadata per record; maintain an **allowlist** (the lab's `ALLOWED_LICENSES`), honour opt-outs and takedowns, separate **PII/secrets scrubbing** from licensing, and record the policy version used. In the lab the `license_filter` stage of `run_pipeline` drops records whose license is `all-rights-reserved` or `unknown`.

![Funnel](figures/01_data_pipeline_funnel.png)

*Figure 1 — The license stage removes the restricted-license copies the crawl was salted with.*

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

1. Record license at acquisition ([9.9](09.09-data-provenance.md)). 2. Map raw license strings to a normalised vocabulary. 3. Apply the allowlist policy (version it). 4. Handle opt-out registries and takedowns. 5. Scrub PII/secrets. 6. Store the policy version in the dataset manifest.

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

A publisher printing a book must check each quotation's permission; a shelf of unlabelled photocopies is unusable.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

The lab's allowlist `{cc-by-sa, mit, cc0, cc-by, public-domain}` keeps wiki/code/chat/web synthetic sources and drops injected `all-rights-reserved` and `unknown` records. (The allowlist is itself `REVIEW_REQUIRED`; real policy is organisation- and jurisdiction-specific.)

### 7. CODE

```python
# Run from lab/: the license policy is a set membership test, applied as a pipeline stage.
import data_prep as dp

raw = dp.make_synthetic_crawl(0, 200)
allowed = [d for d in raw if d["license"] in dp.ALLOWED_LICENSES]
print(len(raw), "->", len(allowed), "dropped:", sorted({d["license"] for d in raw} - dp.ALLOWED_LICENSES))
```

### 8. INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT

```
doc + license string -> normalise -> allowlist / opt-out check -> keep or drop (+ reason logged, policy version recorded)
```

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

Legal and ML jointly own a data-use policy: approved licenses, vendor contracts, opt-out handling, a dataset card per release, and a review gate before any run.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Model cards and procurement questions ("what was it trained on, under what terms?") depend on this. The same licensing issues apply to fine-tuning sets, RAG corpora and synthetic data derived from other models' outputs (check provider terms).

### 11. ADVANTAGES

- Reduces legal risk and enables honouring takedowns.
- Clear policy makes data reviews faster.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

- License metadata is often missing, wrong or inconsistent across mirrors.
- Law is unsettled and differs by jurisdiction; a filter cannot decide legality.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

A strict allowlist lowers legal risk but shrinks data (and may bias the corpus toward a few sources); a loose one maximises data with more risk.

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

- Treating "publicly accessible" as "free to use".
- Dropping license metadata during cleaning.
- No process for takedown requests.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Filtering is cheap; the cost is legal review and, if a license problem is found late, re-training. Settle policy before the run.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Secrets (API keys, credentials) and personal data in code and web dumps are memorised and can be regurgitated: scan and remove before training. Licensing and privacy are separate controls.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

"I'd keep provenance and license metadata per record, apply a counsel-approved license allowlist as a pipeline stage, handle opt-outs and takedowns, scrub PII and secrets separately, and record the policy version in a dataset card. I'd be clear that the filter implements a policy, it doesn't decide legality."

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

Per-record license metadata plus a counsel-approved allowlist, opt-out handling and PII scrubbing.
