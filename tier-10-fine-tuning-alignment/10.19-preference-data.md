### 1. WHAT IS IT?

**Preference data** are examples of the form `(prompt, chosen answer, rejected answer)`: a comparison of which of two responses is better.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

SFT cannot express "A is better than B". Comparisons are easier and more consistent for humans to give than absolute scores or perfect demonstrations.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

Sources: human annotators ranking 2-4 samples; **AI feedback** (a judge model applying a rubric); implicit signals (thumbs-up, edits). Issues: annotator disagreement and noise, length bias (longer looks better), position bias, rubric drift, privacy of logged data. Pairs are most useful when both answers come from (or near) the policy being tuned.

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

Sample k answers per prompt -> label best/worst (or pairs) -> filter ties and low-agreement items -> store `(x, y_chosen, y_rejected)`.

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Taste tests: "which of these two soups is better?" is easier than "write the perfect recipe".

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

Lab: the "chosen" answer is the correct upper-case answer; the "rejected" answer is the sloppy lower-case echo (`sloppy` in [`lab/data.py`](lab/data.py)). Pairwise accuracy later hits 1.00 in the DPO run.

### 7. CODE

```python
pair = {"prompt": "up cat sun", "chosen": "CAT SUN", "rejected": "cat sun"}
```

### 8. INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT

```
prompt --> sample answers --> annotator / judge --> (prompt, chosen, rejected)
```

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

Companies collect thousands of pairs for tone, safety and format, often with rubrics and AI judges plus human audits.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Feeds reward models ([10.21](10.21-reward-models.md)), RLHF ([10.22](10.22-rlhf.md)) and DPO ([10.25](10.25-direct-preference-optimization-dpo.md)).

### 11. ADVANTAGES

Captures relative quality; cheap to label compared with writing answers.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

Noisy and biased; says nothing about *how much* better.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

Human quality versus AI-judge scale.

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

Not controlling length bias; pairs from a different model than the policy; no inter-annotator check.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Labelling cost scales with pairs; judge-model inference cost.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Privacy of logged prompts; poisoned labels can steer the model.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

Preference data are prompts with a chosen and a rejected answer. They're easier to label than demonstrations and let me teach relative quality, but they carry noise, length bias and rubric drift, so I audit them.

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

Preference data = prompt + better + worse.

### 19. INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

**Basic:** What is a preference pair?

**Intermediate:** Where do preference labels come from?

**Advanced:** What is length bias?

**Scenario:** Labels disagree 30 % of the time. What do you do?

### 20. HANDS-ON EXERCISE

Build 20 (chosen, rejected) pairs for a formatting rule and check agreement with a colleague.
