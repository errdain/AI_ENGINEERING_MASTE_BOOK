### 1. WHAT IS IT?

**RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback)** optimises a policy to maximise a reward model's score while a KL penalty keeps it close to a reference model.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

To optimise behaviours that are hard to demonstrate but easy to compare, using online sampling.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

Pipeline: (1) SFT model `pi_ref`; (2) train an RM on preferences; (3) optimise the policy `pi` to maximise `E[r(x,y)] - beta * KL(pi || pi_ref)` with an RL algorithm (classically PPO, [10.23](10.23-ppo-intuition-for-alignment.md)). The **KL leash** stops drift into reward-hacking gibberish. Classic setups need a policy, a frozen reference, an RM and (often) a value model: **four large models' worth** of memory/infrastructure. **RL with verifiable rewards** swaps the RM for a programmatic checker.

![KL penalty and PPO clipping](figures/12_ppo_clip_and_kl.png)

*Figure (analytic): the right panel shows the reward-minus-beta-KL trade-off: a small beta lets the policy drift much further for more raw reward; a large beta keeps it near the reference.*

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

Loop: sample responses from `pi` -> score with RM (minus KL penalty) -> estimate advantages -> PPO update -> repeat.

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

A coach watches a player's practice game, scores it, and nudges strategy, while telling the player not to abandon their fundamentals.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

Not run in the lab (it needs sampling and four models). The lab's DPO is the offline alternative; Exercise 6 builds a tiny RM for best-of-n.

### 7. CODE

```python
# per-sample shaped reward (conceptual)
reward = rm(x, y) - beta * (logp_policy - logp_ref)   # KL penalty estimated per sample
```

### 8. INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT

```
prompts --> policy samples --> RM score - beta*KL --> PPO --> better policy (loop)
```

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

Used by labs for general assistants; most application teams use SFT + DPO-style methods because they are simpler to run.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Historical basis of chat assistants; verifiable-reward RL underlies reasoning-model training (Tier 12).

### 11. ADVANTAGES

Online exploration; can exceed demonstration quality.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

Complex, unstable, expensive; reward hacking.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

Ceiling and exploration versus simplicity (DPO).

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

Too-small beta; trusting the RM beyond its distribution; no KL monitoring.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Four models in memory and online generation each iteration.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

RM exploits and jailbreaks via reward hacking; monitor behaviour not just reward.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

RLHF trains a reward model from preferences and then optimises the policy with PPO against that reward, with a KL penalty to the SFT reference so it doesn't drift or hack the reward. It's powerful but heavy: policy, reference, RM and value model. Many teams use DPO instead.

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

RLHF: reward model plus RL plus KL leash.
