### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Vision evaluation is measuring how well a vision model does its job: task metrics (accuracy, recall@k, mAP, IoU/Dice, CER), robustness, fairness and human review.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

A model that is 95% accurate on its test set can fail on production lighting, devices or subpopulations; metrics must match the decision being made.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

**By task.** Classification: accuracy, per-class precision/recall/F1, confusion matrix. Retrieval: recall@k, MRR. Detection: AP/mAP at IoU thresholds. Segmentation: mean IoU, Dice. OCR: CER/WER, field accuracy. VLM: VQA accuracy, hallucination rates, human preference (REVIEW_REQUIRED for benchmark definitions).

**Beyond averages.** Slice by lighting, device, geography, skin tone, object size; check calibration; test under corruption (blur, noise, JPEG, rotation) and distribution shift.

**Data hygiene.** Avoid near-duplicate leakage between train and test (especially with web-scraped data); hold out *scenes*, not just images.

**Chance baselines.** Always state chance: 25% for the lab's 4 classes; about 8.3% recall@1 for 12 captions.

**Lab evidence.** Accuracy comparisons in this tier are on synthetic data with one seed; they illustrate methodology, not general ranking.

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

Define metric per decision -> build labelled eval set with slices -> baseline and chance -> run -> inspect errors -> gate release ([Tier 10 Fine-tuning & Alignment](../tier-10-fine-tuning-alignment/)-style evaluation, Tier 15).

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Report not only 'accuracy 94%' but 'circle vs cross confusion 9%, worse on noisy images'.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

Lab tests encode evaluation hygiene: `test_classifiers_beat_chance` (accuracy > 0.6 with chance 0.25), `test_retrieval_recall_above_chance_after_clip_training` (recall > 3x chance and > 0.5).

### 8. INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT

Input: model, eval sets, slices. Process: metrics, robustness tests. Output: report + release decision.

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

A hospital requires per-site and per-scanner sensitivity before deploying; a drift dashboard re-evaluates monthly on freshly labelled samples.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

### 11. ADVANTAGES

Prevents silent failures; supports compliance.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

Metrics can mislead (pixel accuracy); labels are noisy; benchmarks saturate and leak.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

Eval cost (labels, human review) vs coverage; single headline metric vs many slices.

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

No chance baseline; test leakage; averaging over unbalanced classes; evaluating at one IoU only; ignoring calibration.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Human review is the expensive part; sample strategically.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Bias audits and privacy of evaluation images; adversarial robustness tests.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

Evaluate vision systems with task-appropriate metrics (accuracy/F1, recall@k, mAP, IoU/Dice, CER), always against a chance baseline, sliced by conditions and subgroups, with corruption/robustness tests and leakage-free splits. Averages hide failures; I gate releases on slice-level thresholds.

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

Task-matched metrics, chance baselines, slices and robustness - not one average.
