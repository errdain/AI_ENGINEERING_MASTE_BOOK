### 1. What is it?
The **ROC curve** plots the true-positive rate (recall) against the false-positive rate as the decision threshold sweeps from high to low. **AUC** is the area under it: the probability that a random positive receives a higher score than a random negative (ties count half).

### 2. Why does it exist?
It measures how well a score *ranks* positives above negatives independent of any single threshold or calibration.

### 3. Detailed explanation
- **Interpretation:** 0.5 = no ranking ability (a constant or random score; tested), 1.0 = perfect separation, < 0.5 = worse than random (flip the sign: `AUC(−s) = 1 − AUC(s)`, tested).
- **Rank-based:** any strictly increasing transform of the scores leaves AUC unchanged (`exp(5s)` gives the same AUC, tested). Therefore AUC says nothing about calibration (2.43).
- **Implementation checks:** our trapezoid ROC-AUC, the Mann–Whitney rank form and scikit-learn agree, including with tied scores.
- **Prevalence-insensitive but also precision-blind.** Same model quality at prevalence 50%, 5%, 0.5%: ROC-AUC 0.978, 0.982, 0.983 (essentially constant). But **average precision** (area under the precision–recall curve) falls 0.978 → 0.830 → 0.458, and at threshold 0.5 precision falls 0.92 → 0.85 → 0.50 with recall 0.92 → 0.67 → **0.04**. For rare positives, use precision–recall curves (average precision), because FPR is dominated by the huge number of negatives.
- **AUC summarizes all thresholds, including irrelevant ones** (e.g., FPR > 20% may be unacceptable). Partial AUC or recall at a fixed FPR matches operations better.
- **Choosing a threshold** requires costs (2.51): pick the point on the curve, not the area.
- **Multiclass:** one-vs-rest AUC per class, averaged.
- **Confidence intervals:** AUC has sampling variance; bootstrap it.
- **Ties and degenerate cases:** need both classes present (`roc_auc` raises otherwise).

### 4. How does it work?
Sort by score, sweep the threshold, record (FPR, TPR) at each distinct score, integrate with the trapezoid rule.

### 8. Input → Process → Output
`scores → threshold sweep → (FPR, TPR) curve → area`

### 9. Enterprise use case
Used to compare risk models across segments and over time; operations then choose a threshold with costs and capacity.

### 10. AI engineering connection
Evaluating rerankers, safety classifiers, hallucination detectors and LLM-judge scores as rankers of good vs bad outputs.

### 11. Advantages
Threshold-free, rank-based, widely understood.

### 12. Limitations
Misleading for rare positives; blind to calibration; averages over irrelevant operating regions.

### 13. Trade-offs
ROC (class-balance-insensitive) vs PR (reflects precision under imbalance).

### 14. Common mistakes
Treating AUC as accuracy; using ROC-AUC alone for rare events; comparing AUCs from different populations; ignoring confidence intervals.

### 15. Performance / cost
`O(n log n)` for sorting.

### 16. Security
AUC does not reveal subgroup disparities; compute per slice.

### 17. Interview explanation
"ROC-AUC is the probability a random positive outranks a random negative. It's threshold-free and rank-based, so it ignores calibration, and with rare positives it can look excellent while precision is poor, so I add precision–recall curves and pick the operating point with costs."

### 18. Rapid-fire
AUC = P(score_pos > score_neg); 0.5 chance; rank-only; use PR-AUC for rare positives.
