# 2.43 Calibration

**Metadata** — Tier: 2 · Phase: 1 · Priority: P0 · Difficulty: 3/5 · Interview: High · Hands-on: High

### 1. What is it?
A probabilistic classifier is **calibrated** if, among predictions with probability `p`, about a fraction `p` are truly positive: `P(y=1 | p̂ = p) = p`.

### 2. Why does it exist?
Decisions use probabilities directly (expected cost, risk scores, abstention). Uncalibrated scores that look like probabilities mislead.

### 3. Detailed explanation
- **Calibration vs discrimination:** discrimination = ranking (AUC); calibration = honesty of the numbers. A model can rank perfectly and be badly calibrated, or vice versa. In the lab, recalibration changes ECE and Brier but **leaves AUC identical** (0.801).
- **Reliability diagram:** bin predictions (`reliability_bins`), plot mean predicted probability vs observed positive rate. The diagonal = perfect.
- **Expected calibration error (ECE):** weighted mean of `|accuracy − confidence|` over bins. Depends on binning; report the bin count.
- **Proper scoring rules** (log loss, Brier score) reward honest probabilities and combine calibration and sharpness. Brier = mean `(p − y)²`; matches scikit-learn.
- **Common failure: overconfidence** in over-parameterized models. Demonstration (40 features, 300 training samples): the raw model predicts 0.09 where the observed rate is 0.14 and 0.91 where it is 0.86 (extremes too extreme); ECE **0.0576**, Brier 0.1851.
- **Post-hoc fixes** (fit on a *held-out calibration set*, never on training data):
  - **Temperature scaling:** divide logits by `T` (found 1.43, i.e. >1 = soften). ECE → 0.0170, Brier → 0.1819.
  - **Platt scaling:** fit a logistic regression on the logit. ECE → 0.0167.
  - **Isotonic regression:** nonparametric monotone fit (needs more data).
- **Calibration under shift:** calibration drifts with prevalence and covariate changes; correct base rates explicitly (2.50) and monitor calibration in production (2.34).
- **LLMs:** token probabilities from instruction-tuned models are often poorly calibrated; verbalized confidence is unreliable; evaluate before using as a risk signal (Tier 15, 30; REVIEW_REQUIRED).
- **Multiclass:** top-label (confidence) calibration or class-wise calibration.

### 4. How does it work?
Collect predictions on held-out data → bin and compare → fit a monotone map from raw score to probability → verify on a separate set.

### 9. Enterprise use case
Credit, insurance and medical settings require calibrated risk estimates for pricing and compliance.

### 10. AI engineering connection
Confidence thresholds for answer abstention, retrieval-score thresholds in RAG, guardrail classifiers, LLM-judge probabilities.

### 11. Advantages
Makes probabilities usable for cost-based decisions; cheap post-hoc fixes.

### 12. Limitations
Needs held-out data; breaks under shift; binning-dependent metrics; class-conditional miscalibration may be hidden.

### 13. Trade-offs
Recalibration is cheap but cannot fix poor ranking.

### 14. Common mistakes
Calibrating on training data; reporting AUC as if it implied calibration; trusting softmax outputs; using too few bins/samples; never re-checking in production.

### 15. Performance / cost
Fitting temperature/Platt is negligible compared with training.

### 16. Security
Overconfident wrong predictions are exploitable; adversarial inputs often yield high confidence.

### 17. Interview explanation
"Calibration means predicted probabilities match observed frequencies. Discrimination, like AUC, is about ranking and is unaffected by recalibration. Large networks tend to be overconfident, so I fit temperature or Platt scaling on a held-out set, check a reliability diagram plus ECE and Brier, and monitor it because shift breaks it."

### 18. Rapid-fire
