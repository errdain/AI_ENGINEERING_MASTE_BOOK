# 2.34 Distribution shift

**Metadata** — Tier: 2 · Phase: 1 · Priority: P0 · Difficulty: 3/5 · Interview: High · Hands-on: High
Prerequisites: 2.31, 1.28, 1.38 · Leads to: Tier 31 Observability/drift monitoring, retraining policies

### 1. What is it?
When the **data distribution at deployment differs from the training distribution**, so performance degrades. Also called dataset shift or drift.

### 2. Why does it exist?
Models are fitted to the past; production is the future. Shift is the normal condition, not an exception.

### 3. Detailed explanation
Types (with `x` = features, `y` = label):
- **Covariate shift:** `P(x)` changes, `P(y|x)` is stable. Example: customers get younger. Harms models that are wrong in the new region of feature space. Demonstration: a *linear* model fitted to `y = x²` on `x ~ N(0,1)` has MSE 2.0 in-distribution but **36.2** when `x ~ N(2,1)`.
- **Label (prior) shift:** `P(y)` changes, `P(x|y)` stable. Example: fraud rate drops from 50% to 5%. With a fixed classifier recall stays about 0.97 but **precision falls from 0.96 to 0.54**: base rates matter (1.34).
- **Concept drift:** `P(y|x)` changes. Example: user preferences or fraud tactics evolve. Needs new labels.
- **Upstream/pipeline changes:** new units, schema changes, missing features, new tokenizer or embedding model.
- **Detection (no labels needed):** compare feature distributions with PSI (0.004 same vs 3.37 shifted), the KS statistic (0.024 vs 0.69), or train a **domain classifier** to tell training from production data: AUC 0.50 means no detectable covariate shift, 0.92 means strong shift. With labels: monitor accuracy/precision/recall/calibration over time slices.
- **Mitigation:** monitor and alert; retrain on recent data (windows, weights); importance weighting for covariate shift; recalibrate thresholds/priors; robust or domain-adaptive training; fall back to rules or human review when inputs are out-of-distribution.
- **PSI rule of thumb** (<0.1 stable, 0.1–0.25 moderate, >0.25 large) is a convention, not a theorem.
- **LLM systems:** prompt distribution changes, new documents in the corpus, model-provider updates, seasonality in queries (Tier 31).

### 4. How does it work?
Log inputs and predictions, compute drift statistics against a reference window per feature and for model outputs, alert on thresholds, and evaluate with fresh labels when they arrive.

### 5. Simple example
A driver trained only on city streets takes the highway.

### 6. Technical example
A recommender trained before a product redesign sees different click patterns after launch; embedding-model upgrades make old vectors incomparable.

### 8. Input → Process → Output
`P_train ≠ P_prod → degraded accuracy/calibration → detect → retrain / adapt`

### 9. Enterprise use case
Drift dashboards, scheduled retraining, canary releases and rollback plans are standard MLOps practice (Tier 31).

### 10. AI engineering connection
Corpus drift for RAG, query-topic drift, tool/API changes for agents, provider model updates, embedding-version mismatches.

### 11. Advantages
(Of monitoring) catches silent failures early.

### 12. Limitations
Unlabeled drift detection says 'different', not 'worse'; labels arrive late.

### 13. Trade-offs
Frequent retraining reduces staleness but adds cost and risk; thresholds trade false alarms for missed drift.

### 14. Common mistakes
No monitoring; alerting on every statistically significant tiny drift; assuming drift always hurts; retraining on contaminated recent data; ignoring label delay.

### 15. Performance / cost
Drift computations are cheap; labeling for ground truth is the costly part.

### 16. Security
Attackers can induce drift (poisoning, adversarial traffic) or hide inside it; monitor segments and inputs, not only aggregate metrics.

### 17. Interview explanation
"Distribution shift means production data differs from training data. I separate covariate shift, label shift and concept drift because the remedies differ: monitor features with PSI or KS and a domain classifier, track prediction and base-rate changes, and retrain or recalibrate when metrics on fresh labels degrade."

### 18. Rapid-fire
