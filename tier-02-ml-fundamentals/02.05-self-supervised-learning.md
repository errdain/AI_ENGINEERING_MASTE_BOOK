# 2.5 Self-supervised learning

**Metadata** — Tier: 2 · Phase: 1 · Priority: P0 · Difficulty: 2/5 · Interview: Very high · Hands-on: Medium
Prerequisites: 2.2, 2.3, 1.33 · Leads to: Tier 6 Embeddings, Tier 8 Transformers, Tier 9 Pretraining

### 1. What is it?
Learning where the **labels are created automatically from the data itself**: hide part of the input and predict it (next token, masked token, missing patch, matching views).

### 2. Why does it exist?
It turns huge unlabeled corpora into supervised-style training signals, which is how foundation models get their general abilities.

### 3. Detailed explanation
- **Pretext tasks:** next-token prediction (decoder LLMs), masked-token prediction (BERT-style), contrastive matching of two views of the same item, image-patch reconstruction.
- **Objective:** usually cross-entropy (1.37) on the hidden part, i.e. maximum likelihood (1.35) of the data under the model. Perplexity is `exp(cross-entropy)`.
- **Why it works:** to predict the hidden part well the model must capture structure in the data (grammar, facts, correlations), so the learned representation transfers to downstream tasks.
- **Scale is the point:** the labels are free, so datasets are as large as the corpus. This is the basis of Tier 9 (pretraining).
- **Caveat:** it learns the *statistics of the data*, including its errors and biases. Alignment and fine-tuning (Tier 10) shape behaviour afterwards.
- **Demonstration (character bigram on a 1,380-character toy text):** the 'labels' are just the next character. NLL falls from `ln 15 = 2.71` (uniform over 15 symbols) to 0.71 (perplexity 2.03); after 't' the most likely next character is 'h'.

### 4. How does it work?
Take raw data → build (input, target) pairs by masking/shifting → train with a supervised loss → reuse the learned representation or model.

### 5. Simple example
Reading a book with some words covered up and guessing them; you learn the language without a teacher.

### 6. Technical example
GPT-style pretraining: for every position, predict the next token given previous tokens (autoregressive factorization, 1.33).

### 8. Input → Process → Output
`raw data → hide/shift to make targets → predict → loss → representations`

### 9. Enterprise use case
Organizations pretrain or adapt models on proprietary unlabeled text/logs, then fine-tune on a small labeled set.

### 10. AI engineering connection
The foundation of LLMs, embedding models and many vision/speech models (Tiers 6, 8, 9, 13, 14).

### 11. Advantages
Uses unlimited unlabeled data; produces transferable representations.

### 12. Limitations
Expensive compute; inherits data biases; objective ≠ truthfulness or helpfulness.

### 13. Trade-offs
Bigger models/data improve loss predictably but cost grows rapidly.

### 14. Common mistakes
Calling it 'unsupervised' without noting the generated targets; ignoring data quality/duplication; assuming low perplexity means factual accuracy.

### 15. Performance / cost
Pretraining dominates cost; efficient data pipelines and mixed precision matter (Tier 4/9).

### 16. Security
Training data can contain private or poisoned content; models can memorize and regurgitate it.

### 17. Interview explanation
"Self-supervised learning creates its own labels from the data, such as predicting the next token or a masked token. That lets us train on huge unlabeled corpora with an ordinary cross-entropy loss, and the resulting representations transfer to many tasks. LLM pretraining is the main example."

### 18. Rapid-fire
Labels come from the data itself (next/masked token); trained with cross-entropy; basis of foundation models.
