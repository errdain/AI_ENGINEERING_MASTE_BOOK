### 1. What is it?
Of all **actual positives, the fraction the model finds**: `recall = TP / (TP + FN)`. Also called sensitivity, true-positive rate (TPR) or hit rate.

### 2. Why does it exist?
It answers 'how many of the things we care about do we catch?', which governs the cost of misses.

### 3. Detailed explanation
- **Conditional probability view:** `recall = P(predicted positive | actual positive)`.
- **Independent of prevalence** (it conditions on the positives), unlike precision, but still depends on the threshold.
- **Threshold trade-off:** recall rises as the threshold falls (0.447 at 0.9 → 0.921 at 0.1 in the lab) at the cost of precision (2.38).
- **Specificity** (true-negative rate) is recall for the negative class; `FPR = 1 − specificity`. ROC curves plot TPR vs FPR (2.41).
- **Where recall matters most:** when misses are costly: disease screening, fraud, safety incidents, security detection, retrieval for RAG (a missing passage cannot be generated from).
- **Recall@k:** share of relevant items appearing in the top k, used for retrieval (Tier 20–21).
- **100% recall is trivial:** predict everything positive. That is why recall is always reported with precision or FPR.
- **Edge cases:** no actual positives in the evaluation set makes recall undefined; report counts.

### 4. How does it work?
Count actual positives, count how many were predicted positive, divide.

### 5. Simple example
Of 40 sick patients, the test found 36: recall 90%.

### 6. Technical example
A guardrail catching 95 of 100 known jailbreak prompts has recall 95%; the 5 misses are the risk to analyze.

### 8. Input → Process → Output
`actual positives → fraction detected`

### 9. Enterprise use case
Compliance and safety teams set minimum recall (e.g., ≥ 99% of known-bad cases) and accept lower precision with human review.

### 10. AI engineering connection
Retrieval recall (does the context contain the answer?), safety classifier recall on red-team sets, tool-call recall, extraction recall.

### 11. Advantages
Direct measure of missed cases; stable across prevalence.

### 12. Limitations
Ignores false alarms; trivially maximized by flagging everything.

### 13. Trade-offs
Higher recall via lower thresholds costs precision and review workload.

### 14. Common mistakes
Reporting recall without precision; evaluating recall on data that excludes hard positives; averaging recall across classes without weights; ignoring subgroup recall.

### 15. Performance / cost
`O(n)`.

### 16. Security
Attackers target the model's recall gaps; maintain adversarial test sets.

### 17. Interview explanation
"Recall is the fraction of real positives we catch, TP over TP plus FN, equal to the true-positive rate. I use it when misses are expensive, always pair it with precision or false-positive rate, and move the threshold to hit a recall target at an acceptable review load."

### 18. Rapid-fire
Recall (TPR, sensitivity) = TP/(TP+FN) = P(flagged | positive); rises as the threshold falls.
