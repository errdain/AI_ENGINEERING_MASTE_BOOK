# 2.51 Metric selection

**Metadata** — Tier: 2 · Phase: 1 · Priority: P0 · Difficulty: 2/5 · Interview: Very high · Hands-on: High
Prerequisites: 2.37–2.50 · Leads to: Tier 15 and 30 Evaluation, Tier 36 Cost engineering

### 1. What is it?
Choosing the **evaluation metric(s) that reflect the real decision, costs and constraints**, plus the threshold or operating point and the uncertainty reporting.

### 2. Why does it exist?
You optimize what you measure. A mismatched metric yields a model that wins the benchmark and loses the business.

### 3. Detailed explanation
Decision guide:
| Situation | Metric(s) |
|---|---|
| Balanced classes, equal costs | accuracy, macro-F1 |
| Rare positives, misses costly | recall at a precision floor, PR-AUC (average precision) |
| False alarms costly (review queue, blocking users) | precision at a recall floor |
| Ranking quality (rerankers, risk scores) | ROC-AUC, precision@k, recall@k, NDCG |
| Probabilities used in decisions | log loss, Brier score, ECE (calibration) |
| Regression, outliers matter | RMSE; robust: MAE, Huber loss; relative: MAPE (careful near 0) |
| Forecasting | compare to naive baseline (MASE-style), prediction intervals |
| Multi-class with long tail | macro-F1, per-class recall |

- **Ranking vs operating point (lab):** a plain and a class-weighted logistic regression have the **same ROC-AUC (0.966) and PR-AUC (0.746)**: they rank identically. At the fixed threshold 0.5, F1 is 0.66 vs 0.46 and log loss 0.082 vs 0.247. Metric choice decides which model "wins".
- **Costs flip the winner.** Expected cost per case at threshold 0.5:
  | cost of miss : false alarm | plain | class-weighted | cheaper |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 : 5 | 0.058 | 0.501 | plain |
  | 1 : 1 | 0.029 | 0.104 | plain |
  | 20 : 1 | 0.437 | 0.194 | weighted |
  With the threshold *tuned to each cost ratio*, the two models are nearly tied (0.037 vs 0.041; 0.0285 vs 0.0285; 0.200 vs 0.193). The differences come from the **operating point**, not the model.
- **Align the offline metric with the business metric** (revenue, resolution time, user satisfaction) and verify with an online experiment (A/B test).
- **Use a small set of metrics:** one primary (decision-linked), a few guardrails (latency, cost, fairness slices, calibration), report uncertainty (bootstrap intervals).
- **Report on slices** (segments, languages, user groups) because averages hide failures.
- **Metric hacking and Goodhart's law:** once a metric becomes a target it stops being a good measure; use held-out evaluation sets and periodic refresh.
- **LLM applications:** task success rate, groundedness/faithfulness, retrieval recall@k, latency and cost per task, human preference win rate, calibrated judge agreement (Tier 30); choose them before building.
- **Decide the metric before running experiments** to avoid selecting the metric that flatters the result.

### 4. How does it work?
Define the decision and its costs → pick primary and guardrail metrics → choose the operating point from costs → set success thresholds vs baseline (2.46) → report with uncertainty and slices.

### 5. Simple example
You judge a delivery service by delivered-on-time, not by how fast the van's engine is.

### 6. Technical example
Fraud: maximize recall subject to review capacity (precision floor) with a calibrated score; RAG: retrieval recall@5, answer faithfulness, p95 latency, cost per answer.

### 8. Input → Process → Output
`business decision + costs → metric(s) + operating point → model comparison → monitoring`

### 9. Enterprise use case
Model cards document the primary metric, its business link, thresholds, slices, and monitoring plan; release gates are metric-based.

### 10. AI engineering connection
Eval-first design for LLM apps: define the golden dataset and metrics before prompts; track quality, cost and latency together (Tier 30, 36).

### 11. Advantages
Keeps work aligned with value; prevents benchmark-chasing.

### 12. Limitations
Real costs are often uncertain; offline metrics only approximate online impact.

### 13. Trade-offs
Single metric (clarity) vs several (coverage); precision vs recall; accuracy vs fairness/robustness slices.

### 14. Common mistakes
Using accuracy by default; choosing the metric after seeing results; ignoring costs and thresholds; one aggregate number without slices or uncertainty; mismatch with online metrics.

### 15. Performance / cost
Some metrics (bootstrap CIs, human evaluation) are expensive; plan evaluation budget.

### 16. Security
Safety and abuse metrics (attack success rate, refusal rate on harmful prompts) belong in the metric suite (Tier 32).

### 17. Interview explanation
"I pick metrics from the decision and its costs: recall at a precision floor for rare, costly events; PR-AUC for ranking under imbalance; log loss or Brier when probabilities drive decisions. Two models can rank identically yet win or lose depending on the threshold and the cost ratio, so I set the operating point from costs, report slices and uncertainty, and confirm with an online test."

### 18. Rapid-fire
Metric = decision + costs + operating point; ranking metrics vs threshold metrics vs probability metrics; costs flip the winner; report slices & uncertainty.

### 19. Interview questions

- Advanced: Derive the cost-optimal threshold for a calibrated model with costs `c_FN` and `c_FP`.
- Scenario: Offline F1 improved 3 points but the A/B test shows no revenue change. Why?
