# 2.15 Regression

**Metadata** — Tier: 2 · Phase: 1 · Priority: P0 · Difficulty: 2/5 · Interview: High · Hands-on: Very high
Prerequisites: 2.2, 1.11, 1.14 · Leads to: 2.17 Loss functions, 2.30 Bias–variance, 2.32 Regularization

### 1. What is it?
Supervised learning where the target `y` is a **continuous number**: price, demand, temperature, latency.

### 2. Why does it exist?
Many business questions are quantities, not categories. Regression predicts the quantity (and ideally its uncertainty).

### 3. Detailed explanation
- **Linear regression:** `ŷ = w·x + b`. Least squares has a closed form `w = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy`, in practice solved by `lstsq` (1.14, do not invert). `mlkit.LinearRegression` matches scikit-learn exactly, including ridge (`ridge=λ`, intercept not penalized).
- **Assumptions behind least squares:** linearity in the features, errors independent with constant variance, no extreme collinearity. Under Gaussian noise, MSE is the maximum-likelihood loss (1.35).
- **Beyond linear:** add nonlinear features (polynomial terms: `poly_features`), or use more flexible models (trees, neural nets).
- **Evaluation:** MSE/RMSE/MAE and `R² = 1 − SSE/SST` (fraction of variance explained relative to the mean baseline). A model with R² ≤ 0 is no better than predicting the mean.
- **Residuals:** for least squares with an intercept their mean is exactly zero. Look at residual plots for structure (curvature, heteroscedasticity).
- **Extrapolation is dangerous:** a fitted line has no knowledge outside the training range.
- **Demonstration (true `y = 2x₁ − x₂ + 0.5` plus noise, 200 train / 100 test):** fitted coefficients 2.01, −0.98, intercept 0.48; test MSE 0.134 vs 5.69 for the mean baseline; R² = 0.976.
- **Count / bounded targets** (counts, rates) often need different losses or link functions; not covered here.

### 4. How does it work?
Features → weighted sum → compare with target through a loss → solve (closed form) or optimize (gradient descent, 2.19).

### 5. Simple example
Drawing the best straight line through a scatter plot to predict height from age.

### 6. Technical example
Predict next-week demand from price, promotion and seasonality; predict query latency from payload size and index state.

### 7. Code
```python
import mlkit as M
X, y = M.make_linear(300, noise=0.5, seed=0)
m = M.LinearRegression().fit(X[:200], y[:200]); pred = m.predict(X[200:])
print(m.coef_.round(2), round(m.intercept_, 2), M.mse(y[200:], pred))           # [ 2.01 -0.98] 0.48 0.134
print(M.mse(y[200:], M.MeanBaseline().fit(X[:200], y[:200]).predict(X[200:])))   # 5.69
```

### 8. Input → Process → Output
`x → w·x + b → ŷ → loss vs y → fitted w, b`

### 9. Enterprise use case
Forecasting, pricing, capacity planning, risk scores; typically paired with prediction intervals and monitoring.

### 10. AI engineering connection
Reward models output scalar scores; latency/cost predictors for routing; embedding-quality calibration; scalar heads in neural nets.

### 11. Advantages
Interpretable (coefficients), fast, strong baseline, convex (1.45).

### 12. Limitations
Linear structure; sensitive to outliers (MSE) and collinearity; no uncertainty unless modelled.

### 13. Trade-offs
Simple models extrapolate smoothly but underfit; flexible models fit better but overfit (2.26–2.30).

### 14. Common mistakes
Reading coefficients as causal effects; extrapolating; ignoring scaling when regularizing; evaluating R² on training data only; using regression for a ranking problem.

### 15. Performance / cost
Closed form costs `O(nd² + d³)`; for large `n`/`d` use SGD (2.20).

### 16. Security
Features that proxy protected attributes yield discriminatory predictions; inspect coefficients and slices.

### 17. Interview explanation
"Regression predicts a continuous value. Linear regression minimizes squared error, which is maximum likelihood under Gaussian noise, and I solve it with a least-squares solver rather than inverting a matrix. I compare against a mean baseline with R² and check residuals for structure."

### 18. Rapid-fire
Predict a number; least squares = MLE under Gaussian noise; R² compares to the mean baseline.

### 19. Interview questions
- Basic: What does R² measure?
- Intermediate: Why is the mean of the residuals zero with an intercept?
- Advanced: What happens to coefficients with highly collinear features, and how does ridge help?
- Scenario: Your model has R² = 0.9 in training and 0.2 in production. What do you check?
