# 2.29 Variance

**Metadata** — Tier: 2 · Phase: 1 · Priority: P0 · Difficulty: 2/5 · Interview: High · Hands-on: Medium
Prerequisites: 2.27, 1.30 · Leads to: 2.30 Bias–variance trade-off, ensembling, 2.44 Cross-validation

### 1. What is it?
In this concept, **model variance**: how much the learned predictions would change if the model were trained on a *different training sample* from the same distribution.

### 2. Why does it exist?
High variance is the mechanism behind overfitting: the model chases the idiosyncrasies of the particular training set.

### 3. Detailed explanation
- **Terminology:** this is the variance (1.30) of the *prediction* `f̂(x)` over random training sets: `Var_D[f̂(x)]`. It is not the variance of the data or of the label noise. Same formula, different random variable.
- **Grows with model flexibility:** (polynomials, 30 training points, noise σ²=0.04) variance 0.0028 (degree 1), 0.0050 (degree 3), 0.0125 (degree 9). For least squares with a fixed design the theory is `σ²·p/n`, with `p` parameters; for degree 3, 0.04·4/30 = 0.0053 (measured 0.0050).
- **Shrinks with data:** degree-3 variance 0.0092 (n=15), 0.0050 (30), 0.00138 (120), 0.00035 (480): roughly ∝ 1/n.
- **With random (rather than fixed) training inputs** extra variance appears from which `x` values were sampled: the first test we ran with random inputs showed degree-1 variance above degree-3. Clean teaching experiments fix the design; real data has both sources.
- **Reducing variance:** more data, simpler model, regularization (2.32), bagging/ensembling (averaging models trained on resamples), dropout, early stopping, feature selection.
- **Diagnosis:** large spread of cross-validation scores across folds, big train–validation gap, unstable predictions after small data changes (2.44).
- **Variance of evaluation** (the noise in your metric on a finite test set) is a different thing from model variance; both matter for decisions (2.13).

### 4. How does it work?
Train the same procedure on many resampled datasets and measure the spread of predictions at fixed inputs.

### 5. Simple example
Ten archers with the same average aim but widely scattered shots.

### 6. Technical example
A deep tree grown to purity: tiny changes in training data change its splits and predictions a lot.

### 7. Code
```python
import mlkit as M, numpy as np
f = lambda x: np.sin(np.pi * x)
print([round(M.bias_variance_poly(d, f)['variance'], 4) for d in (1, 3, 9)])                       # [0.0028, 0.005, 0.0125]
print([round(M.bias_variance_poly(3, f, n_train=n)['variance'], 5) for n in (15, 30, 120, 480)])    # ~1/n
```

### 8. Input → Process → Output
`flexible model + finite data → predictions depend on the sample → spread across training sets`

### 9. Enterprise use case
Stability tests retrain on resampled data to quantify prediction churn, important for models whose outputs drive customer-facing decisions.

### 10. AI engineering connection
Seed-to-seed variance in fine-tuning, variance of LLM-judge scores, prompt sensitivity; report mean ± std over seeds (1.31).

### 11. Advantages
(Of understanding it) explains why ensembles and regularization help.

### 12. Limitations
Hard to measure without resampling; must not be confused with data variance.

### 13. Trade-offs
Lower variance via simplicity increases bias (2.30).

### 14. Common mistakes
Calling 'variance' the spread of the data; trusting a single training run; ignoring fold-to-fold spread; assuming larger models always have higher variance (not always).

### 15. Performance / cost
Measuring variance means multiple trainings: costly, hence often approximated by CV folds or seeds.

### 16. Security
High-variance models are more susceptible to data poisoning and membership inference.

### 17. Interview explanation
"Model variance is how much predictions change across different training samples. Flexible models have high variance, which shows up as overfitting and unstable cross-validation scores. It shrinks roughly like one over n with more data and falls with regularization or ensembling, at the cost of some bias."

### 18. Rapid-fire
Model variance = prediction spread across training sets; grows with flexibility, shrinks ~1/n with data.
