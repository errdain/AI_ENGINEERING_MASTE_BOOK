### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Tokenizer training is the offline process of learning a tokenizer's vocabulary (and merges/probabilities) from a corpus, then freezing and versioning it as part of the model artifact.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

The tokenizer determines cost, context fit, fairness across languages and downstream quality; a vocabulary learned on unrepresentative text is permanently inefficient for the real workload.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

**Checklist (any algorithm):**

1. **Corpus.** Representative of production: languages, code, domain terms, numbers, URLs; deduplicate and sample deliberately (mix ratios shape vocabulary allocation per language).
2. **Normalize** ([6.30 Unicode normalization](06.30-unicode-normalization.md)) and choose **pre-tokenization** ([6.1 Text preprocessing](06.01-text-preprocessing.md)): digits split individually or grouped? whitespace/indentation kept? regex splits?
3. **Algorithm and size.** BPE ([6.12 BPE](06.12-bpe.md)), WordPiece ([6.13 WordPiece](06.13-wordpiece.md)), Unigram ([6.15 Unigram LM tokenizer](06.15-unigram-lm-tokenizer.md)); target vocab size per [6.28 Vocabulary-size trade-offs](06.28-vocabulary-size-trade-offs.md).
4. **Reserve special tokens** ([6.8 UNK](06.08-unk.md)-[6.11 EOS](06.11-eos.md), chat markers) and byte fallback ([6.29 Byte fallback](06.29-byte-fallback.md)) decisions.
5. **Train**, then **measure**: bytes/token or tokens/char and fertility per language/domain ([6.31 Tokenization efficiency](06.31-tokenization-efficiency.md)), fragmentation of numbers/URLs/code, UNK rate, round-trip on random Unicode.
6. **Freeze and version** the tokenizer files; test that `decode(encode(x)) == x`; ship with the model.

Because the tokenizer must exist *before* model pre-training and cannot be changed afterwards without effectively retraining embeddings, this step deserves real engineering care.

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Writing a phrasebook: you pick the phrases to include by listening to the conversations you will actually have; a phrasebook compiled from the wrong conversations wastes pages.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

Training on English-only text gives few merges for Hindi, so Hindi costs several times more tokens (Figure 8); adding Hindi data to the tokenizer corpus before training fixes that (the model then also needs Hindi data).

### 7. CODE

```python
# run inside lab/ : the corpus decides the vocabulary
from mini_bpe import ByteBPE, compression_ratio
english = "the cat sat on the mat and the dog ran to the park " * 40
code    = "def add(a, b):\n    return a + b\n" * 40
t_en, t_code = ByteBPE.train(english, 40), ByteBPE.train(code, 40)
for name, tok in [("trained on English", t_en), ("trained on code", t_code)]:
    print(name, "-> English:", round(compression_ratio(tok, english), 2), "bytes/token | code:", round(compression_ratio(tok, code), 2))
# expected pattern: each tokenizer compresses its own domain better than the other's
```

### 8. INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT

```
corpus ─► normalize ─► pre-tokenize ─► train (merge/prune) ─► measure per language ─► freeze + version ─► ship with model
```

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

A bank training a domain LLM builds a tokenizer on its mix of English documents, tabular text, account numbers and code; it audits how IBANs and amounts tokenize before committing.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Pre-training, continued pre-training and fine-tuning all depend on the tokenizer; vocabulary extension for new domains requires embedding resizing and training ([6.6 Vocabulary](06.06-vocabulary.md)).

### 11. ADVANTAGES

Right-sized vocabulary for your data lowers cost and improves quality.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

Frozen afterwards; sensitive to corpus mix; hard to evaluate before training the model.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

Domain-specific tokenizer (efficient) versus reusing a public tokenizer (compatibility with existing weights and tooling).

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

- Training on unrepresentative data.
- Not reserving special tokens.
- Not testing round-trip on edge-case Unicode.
- Not versioning the tokenizer.
- Evaluating only on English.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Training time grows with corpus and vocab size; efficiency gains compound across every token ever processed.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Training corpora can leak secrets into vocabulary entries (long unique strings become tokens); deduplicate and filter secrets/PII before training; glitch tokens come from corpus artifacts.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

*"Train the tokenizer on a corpus representative of production, with the right normalization and pre-tokenization, choose algorithm and vocab size, reserve specials, then measure compression and fragmentation per language and domain before freezing and versioning it with the model."*

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

Learn the vocabulary from representative data, measure per domain, freeze, version.
