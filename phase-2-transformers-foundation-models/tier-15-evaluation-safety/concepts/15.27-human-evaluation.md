### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Human evaluation uses trained people to judge model outputs (correctness, helpfulness, safety, preference) and is the reference against which automatic metrics and judges are validated.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

For open-ended outputs no automatic metric fully captures quality. Humans are the ground truth, but only if their judgements are reliable.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

Design: clear rubrics with examples, calibrated raters, blind and randomised presentation, pairwise preference or absolute ratings, adjudication of disagreements.

**Inter-annotator agreement**: Cohen's kappa = (p_o - p_e)/(1 - p_e), where p_o is observed agreement and p_e agreement expected by chance. Hand check: raters agree on 8/10 items with 50/50 label marginals: p_o = 0.8, p_e = 0.5, kappa = 0.6. Rough conventions (Landis-Koch; informal): 0.41-0.60 moderate, 0.61-0.80 substantial; treat them as guides. Kappa depends on prevalence; for more than two raters use Krippendorff's alpha or Fleiss' kappa (REVIEW_REQUIRED for choice).

Practice: majority vote with ties handled conservatively, review disagreements to refine rubrics, track rater drift, and size the human sample for the interval you need. The lab's `import_human_reviews` merges reviewer labels with automatic scores, computes kappa and lists examples where humans and the automatic scorer disagree.

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

Write rubric -> pilot -> measure kappa -> revise rubric -> full labelling with overlap -> adjudicate -> compare with automatic scores.

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Two teachers grading essays: if they often disagree, the rubric needs work before the grades mean anything.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

Two raters on 200 outputs: p_o = 0.88, p_e = 0.52, kappa = 0.75; 14 disagreements adjudicated; automatic scorer disagrees with humans on 9% of items.


### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

Vendor labelling teams or internal reviewers run weekly calibrations; low kappa blocks use of the labels.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Human labels validate LLM judges ([15.28 LLM-as-judge concepts and limitations](15.28-llm-as-judge-concepts-and-limitations.md)) and safety classifiers ([15.23 Harmful-output evaluation](15.23-harmful-output-evaluation.md)).

### 11. ADVANTAGES

- Ground truth for subjective quality.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

- Slow, costly, inconsistent, biased (rater background).

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

- Quality vs cost; pairwise (easier) vs absolute ratings (more information).

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

- No agreement measurement.
- Unblinded comparison.
- Using kappa without looking at prevalence.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Cost per judgement times items times raters; use overlap only on a subset.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Reviewers handle sensitive content; protect data and wellbeing.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

Human evaluation is the reference for open-ended quality. I use clear rubrics, blind randomised presentation, calibration, overlap to measure Cohen's kappa (observed minus chance agreement, normalised), adjudication, and use the labels to validate automatic metrics and judges.

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

Rubric, blind, overlap, kappa, adjudicate.
