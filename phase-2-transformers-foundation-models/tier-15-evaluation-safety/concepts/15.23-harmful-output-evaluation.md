### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Harmful-output evaluation measures how often a model's responses contain content that violates a safety policy (for example dangerous instructions, harassment, self-harm encouragement, sexual content involving minors) across categories and severities.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

Safety needs numbers: rates, by category and severity, with uncertainty, that can be compared across versions and gated.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

Components:
- **Prompt sets** per category: direct requests, role-play and indirect framings, multi-turn, benign look-alikes.
- **Labels**: harmful / not, severity (low-high), and refusal quality; produced by trained reviewers with guidelines, kappa tracked ([15.27 Human evaluation](15.27-human-evaluation.md)).
- **Classifiers/judges** for scale, validated against human labels (precision/recall on the harmful class, [15.10 Precision/recall/F1 in model evaluation](15.10-precision-recall-f1-in-model-evaluation.md); judge limits in [15.28 LLM-as-judge concepts and limitations](15.28-llm-as-judge-concepts-and-limitations.md)).
- **Metrics**: harmful-response rate, refusal rate on disallowed requests, over-refusal rate on benign requests, severity-weighted rate, per-category and per-language breakdown, with Wilson or bootstrap intervals.
- Report coverage (what categories and languages are *not* tested).

Rare events need many prompts: with a true rate of 0.1%, 1,000 prompts give about one expected hit; use the rule-of-three bound (3/n) to state what a clean run proves.

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

Sample prompts per category -> collect responses -> automatic triage -> human verify positives and a random sample of negatives -> compute rates + intervals.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

### 11. ADVANTAGES

- Comparable, trackable safety numbers.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

- Label subjectivity; rare events; coverage gaps.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

- Scale (automatic) vs accuracy (human).

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

- Only English prompts.
- Trusting an unvalidated classifier.
- Reporting no interval.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Human review cost scales with positives; sample negatives to estimate classifier misses.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Reviewers see harmful content: provide wellbeing support and minimise exposure.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

I build prompt sets per harm category (direct, indirect, multi-turn, benign look-alikes), label outputs with trained humans, scale with validated classifiers, and report harmful rate, refusal rate and over-refusal per category with intervals and stated coverage gaps.

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

Per-category harmful rate, severity-weighted, over-refusal, with intervals.

### 19. INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

- **Basic:** What metrics do you report?
- **Intermediate:** How many prompts do you need for a rare category?
- **Advanced:** How do you validate a harm classifier?
- **Scenario:** Harmful rate fell but over-refusal doubled; what's your view?
