### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Perplexity is exp of the average negative log-likelihood per token a model assigns to a text. It is the model's effective branching factor: perplexity 20 means it is as uncertain as a uniform choice among 20 tokens.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

It gives a single, label-free number for how well a language model predicts held-out text, used to monitor training and compare models under identical conditions.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

For tokens x_1..x_N: PPL = exp( -(1/N) * sum_i log p(x_i | x_<i) ).

- Lower is better; PPL >= 1; a uniform model over a vocabulary V has PPL = V; a perfect model on that text has PPL = 1.
- Mean NLL in nats is the loss; in bits, PPL = 2^bits.
- It is per **token**, so models with different tokenizers (Tier 6) are not comparable. Bits-per-byte or per-character normalise this.
- Training-set perplexity falls below held-out perplexity when the model memorises ([15.21 Memorization/data-leakage evaluation](15.21-memorization-data-leakage-evaluation.md)).
- Smoothing matters for count-based models: see Figure 1.

Hand check: probabilities (0.25, 1.0) give mean NLL = ln(4)/2, so PPL = exp(ln 4 / 2) = 2.

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

Run the model over held-out text, collect log-probability of each true token given its prefix, average, exponentiate.

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

A weather forecaster who says '50% rain' every day for a coin-flip climate has perplexity 2; one who says '99% rain' on a dry day is heavily penalised.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

Toy bigram model on a 12-word text: held-out sentence of the same grammar has low perplexity; a scrambled sentence is much higher. Real LLM numbers depend on tokenizer and data; do not quote cross-model values (REVIEW_REQUIRED).

### 7. CODE

```python
# run inside lab/
from toy import BigramLM
from metrics import perplexity, perplexity_from_probs
train = "the cat sat on the mat . the dog sat on the log . the cat saw the dog ."
lm = BigramLM(train, alpha=0.1)
print("held-out :", round(perplexity(lm.token_nlls("the dog sat on the mat .")), 2))
print("scrambled:", round(perplexity(lm.token_nlls("mat the . on dog sat the")), 2))
assert abs(perplexity_from_probs([0.25, 1.0]) - 2.0) < 1e-9
```

### 8. INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT

```
held-out text -> p(x_i | prefix) for every token -> mean NLL -> exp -> perplexity
```

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

Pretraining teams track validation perplexity per data domain to detect regressions and over-fitting; data teams use it to filter low-quality text (REVIEW_REQUIRED for specific practices).

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Perplexity links evaluation to the pretraining objective (Tier 9). It says little about instruction-following, factuality or safety.

### 11. ADVANTAGES

- Label-free, cheap, smooth.
- Sensitive to small modelling improvements.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

- Tokenizer-dependent.
- Not aligned with helpfulness, truthfulness or safety.
- Meaningless for models that do not give probabilities.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

- Smoothness and cheapness vs weak link to end goals.

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

- Cross-tokenizer comparison.
- Evaluating on text seen in training.
- Averaging perplexities instead of NLLs.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

One forward pass over the text; cost scales with tokens.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Unusually low perplexity on a specific passage can signal memorisation ([15.21 Memorization/data-leakage evaluation](15.21-memorization-data-leakage-evaluation.md)).

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

Perplexity is exp of mean per-token negative log-likelihood: the model's effective branching factor. It is useful for monitoring and for comparing models on the same tokenizer and test set, but it does not measure helpfulness or safety, and it is not comparable across tokenizers.

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

PPL = exp(mean NLL); lower is better; tokenizer-specific.
