### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Attention patterns: recurring structures in learned attention such as previous-token, local/nearby, first/special-token, delimiter, induction-style copying and diffuse-averaging heads.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

Knowing typical patterns helps interpret heatmaps and motivates efficient attention designs (windows, sinks).

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

Trained Transformers are reported to show these patterns (`REVIEW_REQUIRED` — specific findings vary by model and paper; interpretability material in later tiers is authoritative). The figure below shows **hand-built** illustrations of what heads *can* do, and which (query, key) pairs are computed under different sparsity patterns.

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

1. Collect weights over many inputs.
2. Look for consistent structure per head.
3. Design/choose attention patterns accordingly.

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Different readers develop habits: some look at the previous word, some at the headline, some skim everything.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

Induction heads: a head finds an earlier occurrence of the current token and attends to the token *after* it (`REVIEW_REQUIRED`: reported in interpretability literature).
