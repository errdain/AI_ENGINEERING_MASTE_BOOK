### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Retrieval-enhanced architectures let the model consult external text at inference: RAG (prompt-level), retrieval inside the model (e.g. chunked cross-attention as in RETRO-style models) and nearest-neighbour LMs. REVIEW_REQUIRED for exact designs.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

Parametric memory is stale and costly to update; retrieval adds fresh, attributable knowledge.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

Architecture-level view: where does retrieved text enter? **In the prompt** (most common; needs a long-enough context and the full pipeline of Tier 20 (RAG)), **via cross-attention layers** attending to retrieved chunk encodings, or **at the output** by interpolating with a kNN distribution. The trade-off versus long context: retrieval is cheaper per query but limited by retriever quality.

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

1. Retrieve top-k chunks. 2. Provide to model (prompt/cross-attention). 3. Generate conditioned on them.

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

An open-book exam versus memorising the textbook.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

Prompt-level RAG with 5×500-token chunks adds 2,500 tokens ≈ 312 MiB KV on the GQA-8 archetype (128 KiB/token).
