### 1. WHAT IS IT?

Encoder-only models apply **bidirectional** self-attention (no causal mask) to the whole input and output one contextual vector per token. They do not generate text autoregressively.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

Understanding tasks (classification, tagging, retrieval embeddings, reranking) benefit from seeing both left and right context and need no generation loop or KV cache.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

Trained with masked-language modelling (predict hidden tokens) or contrastive objectives (objective definitions: [Tier 8 Transformer Architecture](../../tier-08-transformer-architecture/README.md), [Tier 9 Pre-training](../../tier-09-foundation-model-pretraining/README.md)). Output heads sit on top: a classification head on `[CLS]`/pooled vector, per-token tagging, or a pooled embedding for search.

![Encoder, decoder and enc-dec masks](../figures/02_encoder_decoder_encdec.png)

*Figure: left panel is the encoder-only mask: everything is green (fully visible).*

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

1. Tokenize + position info. 2. N identical blocks, each: bidirectional self-attention + FFN. 3. Take hidden states: per token (tagging), `[CLS]`/pooled (classification), mean-pooled (embeddings).

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Reading a whole paragraph before underlining the names in it: you may look both backwards and forwards.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

A BERT-base-shaped config (d=768, 12 layers, ~110 M parameters, REVIEW_REQUIRED) encodes a 512-token passage in one parallel forward pass; there is **no KV cache** because nothing is generated.

### 8. INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT

```
tokens → [bidirectional blocks] → H (n × d) → head (class / tags / pooled embedding)
```

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

Document classification, PII tagging, semantic search embeddings, rerankers: small, cheap, easy to serve with high throughput.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Embedding models for RAG retrieval and rerankers are encoder-style (Tier 20); guardrail classifiers (Tier 24) are often encoders.

### 11. ADVANTAGES

Cheap, parallel, strong for understanding tasks; no decode loop; small memory.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

Cannot generate open-ended text; fixed maximum length (e.g. 512 in classic models); fine-tuning needed per task.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

Quality on understanding tasks per FLOP vs inability to generate.

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

Using a generative LLM where a small encoder classifier is cheaper and more accurate; truncating long inputs silently.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Cost is a single forward pass, O(n²·d) attention but with small n and d; batch heavily.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Classifiers are attackable by adversarial inputs; validate with held-out adversarial sets.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

*"Encoder-only models use bidirectional attention and produce contextual vectors, ideal for classification, tagging and embeddings; they skip generation, so they are cheap and need no KV cache."*

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

Bidirectional, understanding-oriented, no generation.
