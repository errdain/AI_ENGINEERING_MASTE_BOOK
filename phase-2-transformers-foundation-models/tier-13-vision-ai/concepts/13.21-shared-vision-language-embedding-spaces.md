### 1. WHAT IS IT?

A shared vision-language embedding space is a vector space in which images and text are both represented, so an image and a caption describing it land near each other and distances are comparable across modalities.

### 2. WHY DOES IT EXIST?

It lets us compare *across* modalities (text query -> images) with plain vector math, and gives language models a way to 'read' visual features.

### 3. DETAILED EXPLANATION

**Alignment vs fusion.** Dual-encoder alignment (CLIP) keeps towers separate and compares at the end; fusion models (VLMs, [13.22 Vision-language models (VLMs)](13.22-vision-language-models-vlms.md)) mix modalities in attention.

**Modality gap.** In practice image and text embeddings often occupy different regions of the shared sphere even after training (an empirically reported phenomenon; REVIEW_REQUIRED for magnitude).

**Geometry.** Normalized vectors on a hypersphere; cosine similarity; the temperature decides how tightly pairs cluster.

**Limits.** Only what captions mention is aligned (our toy ignores position/size because captions say nothing of it).

![Before vs after alignment](../figures/07b_clip_training.gif)

*Look at: the similarity diagonal emerges over epochs - the two towers are being pulled into one space.*

### 4. HOW DOES IT WORK?

Train towers jointly with a contrastive objective ([13.19 Contrastive learning](13.19-contrastive-learning.md)); evaluate by cross-modal recall@k.

### 5. SIMPLE EXAMPLE

The word 'dog' and a dog photo become neighbours; 'dog' and a car photo do not.

### 6. TECHNICAL EXAMPLE

Lab: both towers output 32-d vectors compared by cosine; `retrieval_recall` measures text->image recall; before training about 0.08 (chance) with seeded init, after 1.00 (measured, `run_lab.py`).

### 8. INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT

Input: image and text. Process: separate encoders into one dimensionality. Output: comparable vectors.

### 9. ENTERPRISE USE CASE

Multimodal enterprise search over slides, screenshots and PDFs: one index for text and images avoids maintaining two search stacks.

### 10. AI ENGINEERING CONNECTION

Foundation for multimodal RAG and for VLM projectors that map vision features into the LLM embedding space.

### 11. ADVANTAGES

Cross-modal search with simple geometry.

### 12. LIMITATIONS

Coarse alignment, modality gap, only aligned on seen concepts.

### 13. TRADE-OFFS

One shared index (simple) vs specialized per-modality indexes (accuracy).

### 14. COMMON MISTAKES

Mixing embeddings from different model versions; ignoring the modality gap when thresholding scores across modalities.

### 15. PERFORMANCE / COST CONSIDERATIONS

Dimension d and N drive index size.

### 16. SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Cross-modal inversion/leakage: text can retrieve sensitive images - enforce access control per item.

### 17. INTERVIEW EXPLANATION

A shared embedding space puts images and text in one vector space via joint contrastive training, so cross-modal search is just cosine similarity. It is coarse (only what captions say is aligned) and shows a modality gap, so thresholds should be calibrated per modality pair.

### 18. RAPID-FIRE ANSWER

One vector space where matching images and text are neighbours.
