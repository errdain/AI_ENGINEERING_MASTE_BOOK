# 3.11 Tanh

**Metadata** — Tier: 3 · Phase: 1 · Priority: P1 · Difficulty: 1/5 · Interview: Medium · Hands-on: Medium
Prerequisites: 3.10 · Leads to: Tier 5 RNN/LSTM, Xavier initialization (3.23)

### 1. What is it?
`tanh(x) = (eˣ − e⁻ˣ)/(eˣ + e⁻ˣ)`, an S-shaped function mapping reals to `(−1, 1)`; a rescaled, zero-centered sigmoid.

### 2. Why does it exist?
Zero-centered outputs make optimization better behaved than sigmoid's all-positive outputs, and the steeper slope gives larger gradients near 0.

### 3. Detailed explanation
- **Relation:** `tanh(x) = 2σ(2x) − 1`; derivative `1 − tanh²(x)`, maximum **1.0** at 0.
- **Still saturates:** on [−6, 6] about 50% of inputs have `tanh′ < 0.01`; deep tanh stacks also suffer vanishing gradients unless initialization keeps pre-activations near the linear region (Xavier, 3.23).
- **Where used:** recurrent networks (hidden-state candidates in LSTM/GRU, Tier 5), small MLPs, certain output ranges (−1,1), and tests/teaching. Transformers use GELU/SiLU in the FFN.
- **Xavier/Glorot initialization** was derived for symmetric activations like tanh (3.23).
- **Small-network fits:** in the lab a tanh MLP solves XOR and fits `sin(x)` (3.17).
- **Zero-centered ≠ always better:** for very deep nets, other design choices matter more (normalization, residual connections).
- **Numerics:** `np.tanh` is stable; naive exponential forms overflow for large |x|.

### 4. How does it work?
Squash to (−1,1); backprop multiplies by `1 − tanh²`.

### 5. Simple example
A balanced dimmer: negative to positive.

### 6. Technical example
LSTM cell candidate `c̃ = tanh(W[h,x] + b)` and output `h = o ⊙ tanh(c)`.

### 8. Input → Process → Output
`z → tanh(z) ∈ (−1,1)`

### 9. Enterprise use case
Rarely a deployment decision; matters for legacy recurrent models.

### 10. AI engineering connection
Recurrent state updates, small controllers, bounded outputs (e.g., actions in [−1,1]).

### 11. Advantages
Zero-centered, smooth, stronger gradients than sigmoid.

### 12. Limitations
Saturates; exp cost; vanishing gradients in depth.

### 13. Trade-offs
Better than sigmoid for hidden layers, worse than ReLU-family for deep feed-forward nets.

### 14. Common mistakes
Large unscaled inputs saturating tanh; mixing it with He init; assuming it fixes vanishing gradients.

### 15. Performance / cost
Slightly costlier than ReLU.

### 17. Interview explanation
"Tanh is a zero-centered sigmoid with range (−1,1) and derivative 1−tanh², maximum 1. It trains better than sigmoid in hidden layers, but it still saturates; it lives on in recurrent networks and with Xavier initialization."
