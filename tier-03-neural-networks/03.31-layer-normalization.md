# 3.31 Layer normalization

**Metadata** — Tier: 3 · Phase: 1 · Priority: P0 · Difficulty: 2/5 · Interview: Very high · Hands-on: High
Prerequisites: 3.30, 1.30 · Leads to: 3.32 RMSNorm, Tier 8 Transformer blocks

### 1. What is it?
Normalizes **each sample over its features**: `y = γ·(x − μ)/√(σ² + ε) + β` with `μ, σ²` computed across the feature (last) axis of that sample. Introduced by Ba et al. (2016).

### 2. Why does it exist?
It gives the stabilizing effect of normalization without batch dependence: identical behaviour in training and inference, any batch size, and natural handling of sequences.

### 3. Detailed explanation
- **Batch independence (tested):** the normalized output of a sample is identical whether it is processed alone or within a batch; there are no running statistics and no train/eval difference (`BatchNorm`'s train vs eval output gap on the same input was 4.9; LayerNorm has none).
- **Properties:** per-sample mean ≈ 1e-16 and standard deviation within 1e-6 of 1 before `γ, β`; works on `(B, T, D)` tensors by normalizing the last axis (checked: shape (2, 5, 16)).
- **Backward** (tested vs PyTorch): `dx = (inv_std/D)·(D·dx̂ − Σdx̂ − x̂·Σ(dx̂·x̂))` over the feature axis; `dγ`, `dβ` sum over batch and positions. Our forward/backward match `torch.nn.LayerNorm` to 1e-9.
- **Parameters:** `2D` (γ and β); 12 for `D=6`.
- **Invariances:** unchanged under adding a constant to all features of a sample (mean removal) and under positive rescaling of the sample (shift error 1.8×10⁻¹⁵ in the lab).
- **Placement in Transformers:** original post-norm `LN(x + Sublayer(x))`; modern pre-norm `x + Sublayer(LN(x))` is more stable for deep models (Tier 8). A final LN is often applied before the output head.
- **Gradient effect:** helps conditioning (and in our test improved the first/last gradient ratio of a 20-layer sigmoid net from ~10⁻¹³ to ~10⁻⁹; residual connections did more, 3.25).
- **Cost:** two reductions over the feature axis per token (mean and variance); RMSNorm removes one (3.32).
- **Epsilon and precision:** compute statistics in float32 even when activations are bf16/fp16 (1.40).
- **Other variants:** GroupNorm (groups of channels; good for small-batch vision), InstanceNorm.

### 4. How does it work?
For each token/sample compute mean and variance over features, normalize, scale and shift.

### 8. Input → Process → Output
`x (per sample) → (x−μ)/σ over features → γ,β`

### 9. Enterprise use case
No batch coupling makes serving simple and safe for multi-tenant batching.

### 10. AI engineering connection
Standard in Transformers; its placement (pre/post) strongly affects trainability of deep models.

### 11. Advantages
Batch-independent, same at train and eval, handles variable batch and sequence shapes.

### 12. Limitations
Slightly less effective than BN in some CNN settings; two reductions per token; mean subtraction is optional (see RMSNorm).

### 13. Trade-offs
Robustness and simplicity vs the extra regularization BN's batch noise provides.

### 14. Common mistakes
Normalizing over the wrong axis; forgetting eps; computing in low precision; mixing pre-/post-norm when porting code; applying LN to features that should keep their scale (e.g. attention logits).

### 15. Performance / cost
Memory-bound; fuse with residual add and bias.

### 17. Interview explanation
"LayerNorm normalizes each sample across its own features, so it doesn't depend on the batch and behaves the same in training and inference — that's why Transformers use it instead of BatchNorm. Modern LLMs put it before each sublayer (pre-norm) for stable deep training."

### 18. Rapid-fire
Per-sample normalization over features + γ,β; no batch dependence or running stats; pre-norm in modern Transformers.
