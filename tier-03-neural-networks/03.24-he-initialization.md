# 3.24 He (Kaiming) initialization

**Metadata** — Tier: 3 · Phase: 1 · Priority: P0 · Difficulty: 3/5 · Interview: High · Hands-on: High
Prerequisites: 3.22, 3.23, 3.12 · Leads to: 3.25 Vanishing gradients, deep ReLU networks

### 1. What is it?
An initialization for ReLU-family activations: `W ~ N(0, 2/fan_in)`. Proposed by He et al. (2015).

### 2. Why does it exist?
ReLU zeroes about half of its inputs, halving the second moment of the output; doubling the weight variance compensates and keeps activation scale constant across layers.

### 3. Detailed explanation
- **Derivation sketch:** for `a = ReLU(z)` with symmetric `z`, `E[a²] = ½ Var(z)`. With `Var(z) = fan_in · Var(W) · E[a_prev²]`, constant scale requires `Var(W) = 2/fan_in`.
- **Measured (30 layers, width 256, unit-variance input):** final activation std **0.67** with He; **2.05×10⁻⁵** with Xavier (predicted decay `0.5^(30/2) ≈ 3×10⁻⁵`); 2.7×10⁻²⁹ with N(0, 0.01²). One factor √2 per layer compounds exponentially.
- **Fan modes:** `fan_in` preserves forward variance (default in PyTorch `kaiming_normal_`), `fan_out` preserves backward variance. The sample std of `kaiming_normal_` matches `√(2/fan_in)` to 2%.
- **Leaky ReLU** uses `2/((1+α²)·fan_in)`; GELU/SiLU are close to ReLU, so He is a standard choice.
- **Normal vs uniform variants** exist; variance is what matters.
- **Gradients** are preserved similarly in the backward direction when fan_out mode is used or when layers are similar in width.
- **Interaction with BatchNorm/residuals:** both reduce dependence on exact scale, but He remains the default for ReLU nets; residual branches may be down-scaled or zero-init for the last layer of each block (3.27).
- **Does not guarantee training success:** only sets the initial scale.

### 4. How does it work?
Set std = `√(2/fan_in)` and sample normally; biases zero.

### 5. Simple example
ReLU throws away half the signal, so start with enough extra gain to make up for it.

### 6. Technical example
`torch.nn.init.kaiming_normal_(w, nonlinearity='relu')` is the default recipe for ResNet-style convolutional layers.

### 8. Input → Process → Output
`fan_in → std=√(2/fan_in) → stable ReLU activations`

### 9. Enterprise use case
Part of training recipes; mismatched init after porting code from another framework is a frequent reproducibility bug.

### 10. AI engineering connection
Used in vision and MLP components; understanding it guides initialization of new layer types.

### 11. Advantages
Keeps deep ReLU nets trainable without normalization; simple.

### 12. Limitations
Assumes ReLU-like and zero-mean symmetric pre-activations; approximate.

### 13. Trade-offs
fan_in vs fan_out mode: forward vs backward preservation.

### 14. Common mistakes
Applying He to tanh/sigmoid (too large, saturation); forgetting biases zero; using fan_in of the wrong tensor axis.

None.

### 17. Interview explanation
"He initialization uses variance two over fan-in for ReLU networks because ReLU zeroes half of the pre-activations; with Xavier the activation scale shrinks by about √2 per layer — a thirty-layer net ends around 1e-5 — while with He it stays near one."

### 18. Rapid-fire
N(0, 2/fan_in) for ReLU; compensates for half the units being zero; Xavier would shrink by √2 per layer.
